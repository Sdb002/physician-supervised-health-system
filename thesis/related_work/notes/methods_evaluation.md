# Methods and evaluation: synthetic-label training, monotonic constraints, health recommender evaluation and reporting

Verification note (applies to every entry below): each paper was checked on 2026-11-15/16 against at least one primary record. DOIs were resolved through the Crossref DOI registry (title, authors, year, venue, pages). Health papers were also matched in PubMed (PMIDs given), preprints through the arXiv API, and DOI-less proceedings papers on the official NeurIPS, PMLR or JMLR page. Abstract text was read from PubMed, Crossref, arXiv, OpenAlex or the open full text (PMC or the author PDF). Where only the title and bibliographic record could be confirmed, the entry says so and no content claim is made. Preprints are labelled "preprint". Semantic Scholar's machine-written "TLDR" summaries were not used as evidence.

How the system is described below (from the brief; not searched): synthetic archetypes (168 for food and recipes, 1,120 for exercise); labels from a hand-written, guideline-derived suitability function, with noise added for training; tuned RandomForestRegressor and HistGradientBoostingRegressor (randomized search grouped by archetype), compared with an untuned GradientBoostingRegressor, LinearRegression and the deterministic formula; per-list Spearman against the noise-free label and top-10 overlap on held-out archetypes (about 0.998 food/recipes, 0.984 exercise, against about 0.79 for the formula); monotonic constraints, checked by tests. The project file `D:\physician\CLAUDE.md` adds that a contraindication gate runs before any ranking, so the models only re-rank items the gate allowed.

## Ranked reading list: the 25 most relevant verified papers and guidelines, and how each applies

### Takeaway
The strongest honest framing comes from three areas. Simulation-study methodology (Morris 2019) and metamodel/emulator validation (Degeling 2020) describe exactly what the table measures: fidelity to a known generating function. Weak-supervision work (Snorkel; Wang 2019) is the closest precedent for training on rule-generated labels, and it also shows the missing piece, an independent reference standard. The next evaluation is best designed from DECIDE-AI (especially item 12, human-AI agreement), silent trials, blinded expert rating and automation-bias evidence.

### Cited Findings
Ranks reflect relevance to the paper's three needs: honest positioning, defensible choices, next evaluation. "Applies" says whether the work SUPPORTS, CRITIQUES or GUIDES the method.

- **R1. Morris TP, White IR, Crowther MJ (2019). "Using simulation studies to evaluate statistical methods." *Statistics in Medicine* 38(11):2074-2102. doi:10.1002/sim.8086. PMID 30652356.** Type: peer-reviewed tutorial plus a review of practice. Venue: biostatistics methods journal. What it did: defines simulation studies as "computer experiments that involve creating data by pseudo-random sampling" whose key strength is that "some 'truth' ... is known from the process of generating the data". It offers the ADEMP planning and reporting structure (aims, data-generating mechanisms, estimands, methods, performance measures) and guidance on performance measures and their Monte Carlo estimation. It reviewed 100 simulation articles in volume 34 of the journal and found that simulation studies "are often poorly designed, analyzed, and reported". Applies: SUPPORTS and GUIDES. The archetype evaluation is a simulation study whose estimand is agreement with the labelling function. ADEMP gives the paper a recognised template for reporting the archetype generator, the noise model, the reference (noise-free label) and the metrics. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30652356/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.8086)
- **R2. Degeling K, IJzerman MJ, Lavieri MS, Strong M, Koffijberg H (2020). "Introduction to Metamodeling for Reducing Computational Burden of Advanced Analyses with Health Economic Models: A Structured Overview of Metamodeling Methods in a 6-Step Application Process." *Medical Decision Making* 40(3):348-363. doi:10.1177/0272989X20912233. PMID 32428428.** Type: peer-reviewed methods overview. Venue: medical decision-making methods journal. What it did: describes fitting a statistical or ML "metamodel" to the outputs of a simulation model. Its six steps are 1) choose a technique, 2) simulate datasets "according to a design of experiments", 3) fit the metamodel, 4) assess metamodel performance, 5) run the analysis with the metamodel, 6) verify the results. Applies: SUPPORTS (framing). The trained rankers are, in this vocabulary, metamodels (emulators) of the hand-written suitability function. The 168/1,120 factorial archetypes are a design of experiments, and Spearman against the noise-free label is metamodel-performance assessment (step 4). The paper still lacks a step-6-style verification against something outside the function. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32428428/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0272989X20912233)
- **R3. Ratner A, Bach SH, Ehrenberg H, Fries J, Wu S, Ré C (2017). "Snorkel: rapid training data creation with weak supervision." *Proceedings of the VLDB Endowment* 11(3):269-282. doi:10.14778/3157794.3157797 (arXiv:1711.10160).** Type: peer-reviewed systems/ML paper. Venue: databases (VLDB). What it did: users write "labeling functions that express arbitrary heuristics, which can have unknown accuracies and correlations". Snorkel denoises them "without access to ground truth" (data programming) and trains end models on the result. In a user study, subject-matter experts built models 2.8x faster with 45.5% higher average predictive performance than after seven hours of hand labelling. Across deployments (including the US Department of Veterans Affairs and the FDA), the end models averaged 132% improvement over prior heuristic approaches and came within 3.60% of large hand-curated training sets. Applies: SUPPORTS (training from heuristic, rule-written labels is an accepted paradigm) and CRITIQUES (Snorkel's claims are measured against hand-labelled data, which this system does not yet have). — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.14778/3157794.3157797); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.10160)
- **R4. Wang Y, Sohn S, Liu S, Shen F, Wang L, Atkinson EJ, Amin S, Liu H (2019). "A clinical text classification paradigm using weak supervision and deep representation." *BMC Medical Informatics and Decision Making* 19:1. doi:10.1186/s12911-018-0723-6. PMID 30616584.** Type: peer-reviewed empirical study. Venue: medical informatics journal. What it did: a rule-based NLP algorithm generated the training labels ("weak supervision"). SVM, random forest, MLP and CNN models were trained on them for smoking status and hip-fracture classification at Mayo Clinic and on the i2b2 2006 smoking task, and scored with precision, recall and F1. CNN reached F1 0.92 (smoking) and 0.97 (fracture) and "captures additional patterns from the weak supervision compared to the rule-based NLP algorithms". Stated limitations: CNN was "more sensitive to the size of training data", and the paradigm "might not be effective for complex multiclass classification tasks". Applies: SUPPORTS the rules-to-labels-to-model pipeline in a clinical setting, and GUIDES: the paper earns its claim that the models add something beyond the rules only by evaluating against a reference other than the rules. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30616584/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12911-018-0723-6)
- **R5. De Croon R, Van Houdt L, Htun NN, Štiglic G, Vanden Abeele V, Verbert K (2021). "Health Recommender Systems: Systematic Review." *Journal of Medical Internet Research* 23(6):e18035. doi:10.2196/18035. PMID 34185014.** This is the ~2021 JMIR systematic review named in the brief; verified. Type: PRISMA systematic review. Venue: digital-health journal (JMIR). What it did: reviewed 73 studies that reported both implementation and evaluation of a health recommender for laypersons. Recommended items fell into lifestyle, nutrition, general health-care information and specific health conditions, and most systems were hybrid. "Evaluations of HRSs vary greatly; half of the studies only evaluated the algorithm with various metrics", while others ran RCTs or in-the-wild studies. It derived five reporting guidelines: say who the target user is and to whom recommendations apply; what is recommended and how it is presented; where the dataset can be found; which algorithms were used; and what evaluation protocol was used. Applies: GUIDES (a checklist the paper can follow directly) and CRITIQUES (algorithm-only evaluation is the immature half of the field). — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34185014/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2196/18035)
- **R6. Vasey B, Nagendran M, Campbell B, et al.; DECIDE-AI expert group (2022). "Reporting guideline for the early-stage clinical evaluation of decision support systems driven by artificial intelligence: DECIDE-AI." *Nature Medicine* 28(5):924-933. doi:10.1038/s41591-022-01772-9. PMID 35585198. Co-published in *BMJ* 377:e070904, doi:10.1136/bmj-2022-070904 (PMC9116198).** Type: consensus reporting guideline (two-round Delphi; 123 experts in round 1 and 138 in round 2; consensus meeting of 16). Venue: Nature Medicine / BMJ. What it covers: 17 AI-specific items (28 subitems) plus 10 generic items for early, small-scale, live clinical evaluation, where the AI's output informs care. The BMJ text places preclinical/offline and shadow-mode validation under TRIPOD-AI/STARD-AI and large comparative trials under SPIRIT-AI/CONSORT-AI. Key items: 2 intended use; 4 AI system (version, training population, preclinical performance); 5 implementation (who makes the final decision); 6/13/16 safety and errors; 7/14 human factors and usability "against recognised standards" and learning curves; 10 user exposure; 11 modifications; 12 human-computer agreement ("how often users agreed with the AI, when and why they departed from it"); 17 data and code availability. Applies: GUIDES the next study; item 12 is the formal home for physician edit/override rates. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35585198/); [BMJ version, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9116198/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-022-01772-9)
- **R7. Papastratis I, Konstantinidis D, Daras P, Dimitropoulos K (2024). "AI nutrition recommendation using a deep generative model and ChatGPT." *Scientific Reports* 14:14620. doi:10.1038/s41598-024-65438-x. PMID 38918477.** Type: peer-reviewed system plus evaluation. Venue: multidisciplinary journal. What it did: a variational autoencoder models users' "anthropometric measurements and medical condition", with loss functions built "to align the network with established nutritional guidelines" and an optimizer that adjusts portions to energy needs. It was evaluated on "3000 virtual user profiles and 84000 daily meal plans, as well as 1000 real profiles and 7000 daily meal plans" for appropriateness "in terms of energy intake and nutritional requirements". Applies: SUPPORTS (the closest peer-reviewed nutrition precedent for synthetic user profiles and guideline-encoded objectives) and GUIDES (even this work added real profiles; its reference is still guideline targets, not expert or patient judgement). — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38918477/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-65438-x)
- **R8. Saeb S, Lonini L, Jayaraman A, Mohr DC, Kording KP (2017). "The need to approximate the use-case in clinical machine learning." *GigaScience* 6(5):gix019. doi:10.1093/gigascience/gix019. PMID 28327985.** Type: empirical methods study with simulation and systematic review. Venue: data-science journal. Finding: "the relationship between the training and the validation set should mimic the relationship between the training set and the dataset expected for the clinical use". Record-wise CV "often massively overestimates the prediction accuracy" compared with subject-wise CV, and almost half of the reviewed wearable/smartphone studies used the optimistic record-wise method. Applies: SUPPORTS grouping by archetype, which is the analogue of subject-wise splitting (no archetype's rows in both train and test). — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28327985/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/gigascience/gix019)
- **R9. Caruana R, Lou Y, Gehrke J, Koch P, Sturm M, Elhadad N (2015). "Intelligible Models for HealthCare: Predicting Pneumonia Risk and Hospital 30-day Readmission." *Proceedings of the 21st ACM SIGKDD (KDD '15)*, pp. 1721-1730. doi:10.1145/2783258.2788613.** Type: peer-reviewed applied ML. Venue: KDD. What it did: GA2M intelligible models on two healthcare tasks. On one pneumonia dataset a rule-based system learned "HasAsthama(x) ⇒ LowerRisk(x)", a true pattern in the data because asthmatic patients "usually were admitted not only to the hospital but directly to the ICU". The authors note that models "incorrectly learn that asthma lowers risk, when in fact asthmatics have much higher risk (if not hospitalized)". The abstract stresses that in healthcare "being able to understand, validate, edit, and trust a learned model is important", and that the intelligible model let such patterns "be recognized and removed". Applies: SUPPORTS constraints and inspection. Learned models reproduce data artefacts that contradict clinical knowledge, so encoding known directions (sodium never raises a score) is a defensible safety design. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/2783258.2788613); [author PDF (asthma passage)](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/KDD2015FinalDraftIntelligibleModels4HealthCare_igt143e-caruanaA.pdf)
- **R10. Wang S, Gupta M (2020). "Deontological Ethics By Monotonicity Shape Constraints." *Proceedings of AISTATS 2020*, PMLR 108:2043-2054 (arXiv:2001.11990).** Type: peer-reviewed ML. Venue: AISTATS. Finding: "it is easy for modern machine-learned systems to violate common deontological ethical principles and social norms such as 'favor the less fortunate,' and 'do not penalize good attributes'". Such principles can be built in "by adding shape constraints that constrain the model to respond only positively to relevant inputs". Applies: SUPPORTS. It gives a published rationale for the system's constraints as stated norms ("more of a beneficial nutrient never lowers a score") rather than only as accuracy devices. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v108/wang20e.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.11990)
- **R11. Gupta M, Cotter A, Pfeifer J, Voevodski K, Canini K, Mangylov A, Moczydlowski W, van Esbroeck A (2016). "Monotonic Calibrated Interpolated Look-Up Tables." *Journal of Machine Learning Research* 17(109):1-47 (arXiv:1505.06378).** Type: peer-reviewed ML methods. Venue: JMLR. Finding: "guaranteed monotonicity of the learned function can be critical to user trust". It trains monotonic look-up tables by a convex problem with linear inequality constraints, and case studies with five to sixteen features "achieve state-of-the-art accuracy on practical problems while providing greater transparency". Applies: SUPPORTS (seminal reference that monotonicity costs little accuracy and increases trust). — [JMLR](https://jmlr.org/papers/v17/15-243.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.06378)
- **R12. Sun Y, Zhou J, Ji M, Pei L, Wang Z (2023). PMID 36656630.** Type: PRISMA-ScR scoping review. Venue: JMIR. Findings: 51 studies; 23 hybrid systems; only 10/51 (19.6%) "considered personal preferences of end users in the design or development"; 62.7% (32/51) used "offline evaluations using computational methods (no user)" and 33.3% (17/51) included end users. - **R13. van Breugel B, Qian Z, van der Schaar M (2023). "Synthetic Data, Real Errors: How (Not) to Publish and Use Synthetic Data." *Proceedings of ICML 2023*, PMLR 202:34793-34808 (arXiv:2305.09235).** Type: peer-reviewed ML. Venue: ICML. Finding: "the naive synthetic data approach -- using synthetic data as if it is real -- leads to downstream models and analyses that do not generalize well to real data". Errors are largest for minority classes and low-density regions. Applies: CRITIQUES. Its synthetic data are generated from real data, unlike this system's rule-generated archetypes, but the warning carries over: scores on synthetic data say little about behaviour on real patients, especially rare condition combinations. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/van-breugel23a.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09235)
- **R14. Chen J, Chun D, Patel M, Chiang E, James J (2019). PMID 30871520.** Companion: **Walonoski J, Kramer M, Nichols J, et al. (2018). PMID 29025144.** Type: validation study; software/methods paper. Venue: medical informatics. Synthea "simulates the lifespans of synthetic patients" with modules for the 10 most frequent reasons for primary care encounters and the 10 highest-morbidity chronic conditions in the US. What it did: defines the silent trial as evaluating an AI model "on prospective patients in real-time, while the end-users (i.e., clinicians) are blinded to predictions". - **R17. Roberts DR, Bahn V, Ciuti S, Boyce MS, et al. (2017). - **R18. Webber W, Moffat A, Zobel J (2010). "A similarity measure for indefinite rankings." *ACM Transactions on Information Systems* 28(4):20. doi:10.1145/1852102.1852106.** Companion: **Järvelin K, Kekäläinen J (2002). RBO handles lists "even when they are incomplete or have only some members in common". ## Training on synthetic data or rule-generated labels: precedents, justifications, stated limitations, critiques

### Takeaway
Training on labels produced by rules or knowledge bases is well established (data programming, ontology-driven weak supervision, rule distillation, informed ML). In every peer-reviewed precedent found, though, the claim of value rests on evaluation against an independent reference (hand labels, real profiles, real outcomes). The synthetic-data literature is consistent that synthetic or simulated patients reproduce their generator's assumptions and miss real variation in care and outcomes.

### Cited Findings
**Rule- and knowledge-generated labels (precedents and how they were justified)**
- Snorkel's justification is cost: labelling is "the largest bottleneck". Heuristic labelling functions with "unknown accuracies and correlations" are denoised without ground truth, and end models come "within an average 3.60%" of large hand-curated training sets. That claim is measured against hand-labelled data. — [Ratner et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.14778/3157794.3157797)
- In clinical NLP, a rule-based algorithm generated all training labels. CNN models "capture additional patterns" beyond the rules (F1 0.92/0.97). Stated limitations: sensitivity to training-set size and possible failure on complex multiclass tasks. — [Wang Y et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30616584/)
- Trove used "medical ontologies and expert-generated rules" as weak supervision for clinical entity classification. It is justified as "easy to share and modify" (no hand-labelled notes, a privacy advantage) while "offering performance comparable to learning from manually labeled training data" on six benchmark tasks. — [Fries et al. 2021, *Nature Communications* 12:2017, doi:10.1038/s41467-021-22328-4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33795682/)
- Rule distillation: an "iterative distillation method that transfers the structured information of logic rules into the weights of neural networks" gave "substantial improvements" with "a few highly intuitive rules" in sentiment analysis and NER. — [Hu Z, Ma X, Liu Z, Hovy E, Xing E 2016, ACL, doi:10.18653/v1/P16-1228](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/P16-1228)
- Knowledge distillation (preprint): Hinton, Vinyals and Dean showed that an ensemble's knowledge can be compressed into a single model by training on its outputs. — [arXiv:1503.02531, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02531)
- Informed ML survey: motivated by "insufficient training data", it classifies approaches by knowledge source, representation and integration point. It notes that "algebraic equations, logic rules, or simulation results can be used in learning systems". Published in IEEE TKDE 35:614-633 (2023) per its Semantic Scholar record; Crossref holds the 2021 early-access DOI record. — [von Rueden et al., doi:10.1109/TKDE.2021.3079836](https://doi.org/10.1109/TKDE.2021.3079836); [arXiv:1903.12394](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.12394)
- Metamodels (ML emulators of simulation models) are validated by simulating datasets "according to a design of experiments", fitting, assessing metamodel performance, and then "verification of the results". — [Degeling et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32428428/)
- Knowledge-based and constraint-based recommendation is an established recommender family. Felfernig and Burke introduce "a taxonomy of recommendation knowledge sources and algorithmic approaches" and discuss constraint-based techniques for complex products. — [Felfernig & Burke 2008, ICEC, doi:10.1145/1409540.1409544](https://doi.org/10.1145/1409540.1409544)

**Synthetic patients and users (precedents)**
- Synthea simulates lifespans with disease and standard-of-care modules. It is justified by freedom from "legal, privacy, security, and intellectual property restrictions" and lower barriers "for promising early-stage developments". — [Walonoski et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29025144/)
- A nutrition recommender with guideline-aligned loss functions was evaluated on 3,000 virtual user profiles (84,000 daily plans) plus 1,000 real profiles (7,000 plans). — [Papastratis et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38918477/)
- A UK primary-care synthetic generator (resampling, graphical models, latent variables, outlier analysis) produced data "not significantly different from original ground truth data" in distributions, dependencies and classifier sensitivity statistics, with low re-identification risk. That generator is learned from real data, unlike rule-generated archetypes. — [Tucker A, Wang Z, Rotalinti Y, Myles P 2020, *npj Digital Medicine* 3:147, doi:10.1038/s41746-020-00353-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-020-00353-9)
- A narrative review lists seven health-care uses of synthetic data, including "hypothesis, methods, and algorithm testing" and "health IT development". It concludes that "the original real data remains the preferred choice". — [Gonzales A, Guruswamy G, Smith SR 2023, *PLOS Digital Health* 2(1):e0000082, doi:10.1371/journal.pdig.0000082](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pdig.0000082)
- Synthetic users for recommender evaluation (preprint): RecSim is "a configurable platform for authoring simulation environments for recommender systems" that supports sequential user interaction. — [Ie et al. 2019, arXiv:1909.04847, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.04847)
- Simulation evidence on offline metrics (preprint): across eleven recommenders in six simulated environments, offline metrics were correlated with online performance, but "improvements in offline metrics lead to diminishing returns in online performance". The ranking of recommenders varied with the amount of initial offline data. — [Krauth et al. 2020, arXiv:2011.07931, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.07931)

**Critiques and stated limitations**
- Synthea "is quite reliable in modeling demographics" but limited for outcomes. Generators "do not currently model for deviations in care" (for example 0% blood-pressure control after hypertension diagnosis against about 70-75% in real data). — [Chen J et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30871520/)
- Using synthetic data "as if it is real" yields models and analyses that "do not generalize well to real data", worst for minority classes and low-density regions. — [van Breugel et al. 2023, ICML](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/van-breugel23a.html)
- Medical AI is "as effective or as robust as the data they are supplied with". The authors recommend grounding simulation-based synthetic data in "clinical reference standards", scoring synthetic data on fidelity, diversity and authenticity, and keeping human-in-the-loop expert review. — [Chen RJ, Lu MY, Chen TY, Williamson DFK, Mahmood F 2021, *Nature Biomedical Engineering* 5:493-497, doi:10.1038/s41551-021-00751-8](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9353344/)
- "Higher stakes, potential liabilities, and healthcare practitioner distrust make clinical use of synthetic data difficult"; data quality and bias limit applicability. — [Giuffrè M, Shung DL 2023, *npj Digital Medicine* 6:186, doi:10.1038/s41746-023-00927-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-023-00927-3)
- Simulation research on recommenders (preprint position paper) has "no agreed upon scientific norms or standards". "High heterogeneity in approaches and low transparency in methods" limit "interpretability, generalizability, and replicability"; the authors urge adopting empirical-research standards. — [Winecoff AA, Sun M, Lucherini E, Narayanan A 2021, arXiv:2107.14333, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.14333)
- Simulation studies in medical statistics "are often poorly designed, analyzed, and reported". ADEMP is the proposed fix. — [Morris et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30652356/)

### Inferences
- Precedents justify rule-generated labels on three grounds the paper can borrow honestly: no labelled data exist yet (cold start), privacy (no real patients), and transparency (labels traceable to cited guidance). None of the precedents treats agreement with the labelling rules as evidence of real-world quality.
- The system's result is best called "emulation fidelity" or "label-function recovery". In Morris's terms the estimand is agreement with g(x), the noise-free suitability function. In Degeling's terms the models are validated metamodels of g. Both framings make the 0.998/0.984 figures non-controversial, because they claim nothing beyond fidelity.
- Unlike Snorkel or Trove, the labelling function here is deterministic and fully known. A reviewer will ask what the ML adds over computing g directly. Defensible answers, each needing an explicit statement in the paper (inference, not from a source): (a) the trained model is the component that can later be updated from physician-review labels (the project's stated future work), with g serving as a knowledge-based prior, in the spirit of informed ML; (b) it shows the pipeline can learn guideline structure from noisy labels under monotone constraints before real labels arrive. If g is also used at run time, say so. If the shipped ranker is the model, report where it disagrees with g.
- The archetypes encode only what the rules encode (no non-adherence, mixed or borderline lab values, missing data, local preference). That is the same limitation Chen J et al. found for Synthea, and the paper should state it in those terms.

### Gaps
- I did not find a peer-reviewed study that trained a model purely on guideline-derived labels for diet ranking and then measured how far it moved away from the guideline after real feedback. The "rules as prior, then fine-tune" path is supported only in general terms (weak supervision, informed ML).
- Classic "learning from expert systems" papers (for example knowledge-based neural networks, Towell & Shavlik 1994, *Artificial Intelligence* 70:119-165, doi:10.1016/0004-3702(94)90105-8) were verified bibliographically only; their content was not read here, so no claims are made about them.

## Monotonic and shape constraints for safe, interpretable healthcare ML

### Takeaway
Monotonic constraints are well supported as a way to encode known directions of effect, to build trust and to prevent clinically absurd learned patterns. They have a published ethical rationale ("do not penalize good attributes") and recent clinical use in gradient boosting. Their limits are just as documented. In scikit-learn they are per-feature and global ("only marginally" constraining effects), unavailable in the plain GradientBoostingRegressor, and unable to express condition-dependent directions. The paper should therefore present them as a guarantee about model shape, not as evidence of clinical correctness.

### Cited Findings
- "Guaranteed monotonicity of the learned function can be critical to user trust". Monotonic calibrated lattices achieve state-of-the-art accuracy on real problems "while providing greater transparency". — [Gupta et al. 2016, JMLR 17(109)](https://jmlr.org/papers/v17/15-243.html)
- Deep lattice networks learn models "monotonic with respect to a user-specified set of inputs". "Six-layer monotonic deep lattice networks achieve state-of-the art performance for classification and regression with monotonicity guarantees". — [You S, Ding D, Canini K, Pfeifer J, Gupta M 2017, NeurIPS 30](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/hash/464d828b85b0bed98e80ade0a5c43b0f-Abstract.html); [arXiv:1709.06680](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06680)
- Monotonicity "with respect to a subset of the inputs" addresses "fairness, interpretability, and generalization". Monotonicity of piecewise-linear networks can be certified by mixed-integer linear programming. — [Liu X, Han X, Zhang N, Liu Q 2020, "Certified Monotonic Neural Networks", NeurIPS 33 (listed in the 2020 proceedings index)](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020); [arXiv:2011.10219](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.10219)
- Seminal: Sill J (1997), "Monotonic Networks", NIPS 10, is listed in the 1997 proceedings index (title and author verified; content not read). — [NeurIPS 1997 proceedings](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/1997)
- Shape constraints can encode norms such as "do not penalize good attributes" by constraining "the model to respond only positively to relevant inputs". — [Wang & Gupta 2020, AISTATS, PMLR 108:2043-2054](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v108/wang20e.html)
- Clinical motivation: a learned model concluded asthma lowers pneumonia mortality risk because asthmatic patients got ICU care; models "incorrectly learn that asthma lowers risk". Healthcare needs models one can "understand, validate, edit, and trust". — [Caruana et al. 2015, KDD](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/KDD2015FinalDraftIntelligibleModels4HealthCare_igt143e-caruanaA.pdf)
- Recent clinical gradient boosting with "prespecified monotonic constraints" (Medically Constrained Gradient Boosting, 849 upper-GI bleeding patients at 3 hospitals): a cross-site hold-out design, AUROC 0.97, AUPRC 0.91; the authors state "external validation in additional settings is warranted". — [Li X, He Y, Hou M, et al. 2026, *JMIR Medical Informatics* 14:e83889, doi:10.2196/83889](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42550984/)
- Interpretable-by-design models are preferable to post hoc explanation of black boxes for high-stakes decisions. — [Rudin C 2019, *Nature Machine Intelligence* 1:206-215, doi:10.1038/s42256-019-0048-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-019-0048-x)
- Medical scoring systems can be learned with "a wide range of operational constraints" (SLIM, used for sleep-apnoea screening at MGH). — [Ustun B, Rudin C 2016, *Machine Learning* 102:349-391, doi:10.1007/s10994-015-5528-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10994-015-5528-6)
- Supporting documentation (not papers):
  - scikit-learn HistGradientBoostingRegressor has `monotonic_cst` (1 increase, 0 none, -1 decrease), added in version 0.23, with dict-by-feature-name support from 1.2. — [scikit-learn HGBR docs](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.ensemble.HistGradientBoostingRegressor.html)
  - RandomForestRegressor gained `monotonic_cst` in version 1.4 and does not support it for multi-output regression. — [scikit-learn RFR docs](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.ensemble.RandomForestRegressor.html)
  - The GradientBoostingRegressor page mentions monotonic constraints only as a feature of HistGradientBoostingRegressor; its parameter list has no `monotonic_cst`. — [scikit-learn GBR docs](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.ensemble.GradientBoostingRegressor.html)
  - LinearRegression has only `positive=True`, which "forces the coefficients to be positive" (all of them). — [scikit-learn LinearRegression docs](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.linear_model.LinearRegression.html)
  - The user guide states that "monotonic constraints only marginally constrain feature effects on the output". They cannot enforce a joint constraint such as F(x1,x2) ≤ F(x1',x2'), are not supported for multiclass classification, and cannot be applied to categorical features. — [scikit-learn user guide, Monotonic Constraints](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/ensemble.html#monotonic-constraints)
- Library papers that the paper can cite for gradient boosting itself: XGBoost — [Chen T, Guestrin C 2016, KDD, doi:10.1145/2939672.2939785](https://doi.org/10.1145/2939672.2939785); LightGBM — [Ke G et al. 2017, NeurIPS 30](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/hash/6449f44a102fde848669bdd9eb6b76fa-Abstract.html).

### Inferences
- Baseline confound (from the documentation above): the untuned GradientBoostingRegressor cannot carry `monotonic_cst`, and LinearRegression can only force all coefficients positive. So "shipped constrained model versus GBR/linear baseline" mixes three things: constraint, tuning and model class. The paper should either add a tuned, unconstrained HistGradientBoostingRegressor as an ablation or state the confound plainly. Dacrema et al. (below) show why tuned simple baselines matter.
- Because the labels come from g, the constraints help only if g itself is monotone in each constrained feature for every archetype. If g penalises a nutrient for some archetypes (for example kidney disease or pregnancy limits), a global "never lowers" constraint biases the model there. sklearn constraints are per-feature and global, so condition-dependent directions need condition-specific features (for example "potassium × kidney flag") or leaving those features unconstrained. The project's description says a contraindication gate removes contraindicated items before ranking, which narrows but does not remove this concern for within-limit amounts.
- The monotonicity tests show the model honours the constraint. They cannot show the constraint is clinically right; that needs the cited-guideline justification per feature. The paper should table each constrained feature, its sign and the guideline behind it.
- Caruana's asthma case is about confounded real data. With synthetic labels the analogous risk is extrapolation in sparse regions (unusual nutrient combinations). The honest claim is that the constraints keep shape guarantees outside the training distribution, not that they make the model correct there.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed study that applies monotone constraints specifically to food or diet ranking.
- XGBoost/LightGBM documentation on monotone constraints was not opened in this session; cite the scikit-learn docs, which were checked, or verify the others before use.
- Content of Daniels & Velikova 2010 ("Monotone and partially monotone neural networks", IEEE TNN 21(6):906-917, doi:10.1109/TNN.2010.2044803) and Cano et al. 2019 ("Monotonic classification: an overview on algorithms, performance measures and data sets", *Neurocomputing* 341:168-182, doi:10.1016/j.neucom.2019.02.024) was not read; only their bibliographic records were verified.

## How health recommender systems are evaluated: reviews, metrics, grouped validation, offline versus online

### Takeaway
Reviews of health recommenders agree that most studies evaluate only the algorithm offline (half in De Croon 2021; 62.7% in Sun 2023), that this is a recognised weakness, and that user-centred evaluation is needed. General recommender research shows offline accuracy rankings can contradict online results. For ranking metrics, Spearman per list is reasonable, but the standard top-weighted measures are nDCG and RBO. For validation, the split must mirror the intended use (subject- or archetype-wise), and tuning must not leak into the test set.

### Cited Findings
**Health recommender reviews**
- 73 HRS studies: half evaluated only the algorithm with metrics. The review proposes five reporting guidelines: target user, what is recommended and how it is presented, where the dataset is, which algorithms, which evaluation protocol. — [De Croon et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34185014/)
- 51 HRS studies: 62.7% offline-only, 33.3% with end users, 19.6% considered end-user preferences in design. The review calls for user-centred evaluation and involvement of health-care professionals. — [Sun et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36656630/)
- A review of health-care recommenders (state of the art and research issues) is available as background. — [Tran TNT, Felfernig A, Trattner C, Holzinger A, *J Intell Inf Syst* 57:171-201 (2021; online Dec 2020), doi:10.1007/s10844-020-00633-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10844-020-00633-6)
- Other verified overviews (bibliographic record verified; content not read):
  - Schäfer H, Hors-Fraile S, et al., "Towards Health (Aware) Recommender Systems", DH '17, pp. 157-161. — [doi:10.1145/3079452.3079499](https://doi.org/10.1145/3079452.3079499)
  - Hors-Fraile S, et al., "Analyzing recommender systems for health promotion using a multidisciplinary taxonomy: A scoping review", *Int J Med Inform* 114:143-155 (2018). — [doi:10.1016/j.ijmedinf.2017.12.018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijmedinf.2017.12.018)
  - Trattner C, Elsweiler D, "Food Recommender Systems: Important Contributions, Challenges and Future Research Directions", 2017. — [arXiv:1711.02760, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.02760)

**General recommender evaluation frameworks**
- FEVR (Framework for Evaluating Recommender systems): "comprehensive evaluation of a recommender system frequently requires considering multiple facets and perspectives". — [Zangerle E, Bauer C 2023, *ACM Computing Surveys* 55(8), doi:10.1145/3556536](https://doi.org/10.1145/3556536)
- 57 evaluation-research papers (2017-2022): offline experimentation predominates; precision, nDCG and recall dominate the metrics; "beyond-accuracy qualities are rarely assessed". — [Bauer C, Zangerle E, Said A 2024, *ACM TORS* 2(1), doi:10.1145/3629170](https://doi.org/10.1145/3629170)
- Accuracy metrics "collapsed roughly into three equivalence classes"; metrics within a class were strongly correlated. — [Herlocker JL, Konstan JA, Terveen LG, Riedl JT 2004, *ACM TOIS* 22(1):5-53, doi:10.1145/963770.963772](https://doi.org/10.1145/963770.963772)
- "The recommendations that are most accurate according to the standard metrics are sometimes not the recommendations that are most useful to users." — [McNee SM, Riedl J, Konstan JA 2006, CHI EA, doi:10.1145/1125451.1125659](https://doi.org/10.1145/1125451.1125659)
- The user-centric framework links objective system aspects to user behaviour through perceived quality and experience. It was validated with four field trials and two controlled experiments (SEM), and perceived recommendation quality and variety were key mediators. — [Knijnenburg BP et al. 2012, *UMUAI* 22:441-504, doi:10.1007/s11257-011-9118-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11257-011-9118-4)
- ResQue is a validated user-experience questionnaire for recommenders: 32 questions, 15 constructs. — [Pu P, Chen L, Hu R 2011, RecSys, doi:10.1145/2043932.2043962](https://doi.org/10.1145/2043932.2043962)
- Baselines: of 18 neural top-n algorithms, only 7 could be reproduced, and 6 of those were often beaten by "comparably simple heuristic methods". The remaining one "did not consistently outperform a well-tuned non-neural linear ranking method". — [Ferrari Dacrema M, Cremonesi P, Jannach D 2019, RecSys, doi:10.1145/3298689.3347058](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.06902)
- Reproducibility guidelines tie recommender experimentation to accountability. — [Bellogín A, Said A 2021, *UMUAI* 31:941-977, doi:10.1007/s11257-021-09302-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11257-021-09302-x)

**Offline versus online**
- Offline accuracy rankings "clearly contradict" online results with the same users. — [Rossetti et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1145/2959100.2959176)
- Offline and online results "often contradict each other". — [Beel et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1145/2532508.2532511)
- Preprint: simulated offline metrics correlate with online performance, with diminishing returns. — [Krauth et al. 2020, arXiv:2011.07931](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.07931)

**Ranking metrics**
- nDCG: graded relevance, rank discount, normalisation to the ideal ordering; it "allow[s] testing of statistical significance of effectiveness differences". — [Järvelin & Kekäläinen 2002](https://doi.org/10.1145/582415.582418)
- RBO: top-weighted similarity for incomplete, non-conjoint rankings, with a tunable top-weight parameter and handling of ties. — [Webber et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1145/1852102.1852106)
- Distance measures between top-k lists (some metrics, some "almost" metrics). — [Fagin R, Kumar R, Sivakumar D 2003, *SIAM J Discrete Math* 17(1):134-160, doi:10.1137/S0895480102412856](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0895480102412856)
- A clinical-order recommender reported precision at 10 (33% to 38% over a most-common-orders benchmark) and noted that "different evaluation metrics satisfy different goals". — [Chen JH, Podchiyska T, Altman RB 2016, OrderRex, *JAMIA* 23(2):339-348, doi:10.1093/jamia/ocv091](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocv091)

**Grouped and leave-group-out validation**
- Subject-wise versus record-wise CV: record-wise "often massively overestimates" accuracy. — [Saeb et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28327985/)
- Block CV is appropriate for predicting to new data or predictor space, but blocking can induce extrapolation; the blocking should match the modelling goal. — [Roberts et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1111/ecog.02881)
- Tuning leak: the CV error of an optimised classifier "was found to be a substantially biased estimate of the true error". A nested CV (inner loop for tuning, outer for error) is the fix. — [Varma S, Simon R 2006, *BMC Bioinformatics* 7:91, doi:10.1186/1471-2105-7-91](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-7-91)

### Inferences
- Per-list Spearman against the noise-free label is a reasonable fidelity metric. A reviewer will expect a top-weighted measure because patients see only the top of the list: nDCG@10 with g as graded relevance, and RBO between the model and g lists. They will also expect the per-archetype distribution (median, worst decile, worst archetype), not just the mean.
- If the randomized search's grouped CV used the same archetypes later reported as held out, the reported figures carry the optimism Varma & Simon describe. The paper should state that test archetypes were untouched during tuning (or use nested CV).
- Holding out single archetypes from a full factorial means each held-out archetype's intake group and each of its findings appeared in training in other combinations, so the test is interpolation. Roberts et al. support adding stricter blocks: leave-one-finding-combination-out, leave-one-intake-group-out, leave-one-condition-out for each of anaemia, hypertension, diabetes, high cholesterol and kidney disease. A leave-items-out test (new foods or recipes) also fits, since the catalogue will grow.
- Beyond-accuracy and safety metrics are the missing columns: the share of top-10 items that exceed a per-archetype limit (sodium, for example), diversity across the week, and coverage of local foods. Bauer et al. report that such beyond-accuracy qualities are rarely assessed, and DECIDE-AI items 6 and 13 ask for error and safety reporting.

### Gaps
- No HRS-specific study comparing offline metrics with clinician judgement for diet ranking was found.
- No published standard exists for "top-k overlap" as used here; RBO and nDCG are the citable choices.

## Reporting guidelines that apply at this stage, and the items a reviewer will expect

### Takeaway
The current paper is preclinical and in silico, so TRIPOD+AI is the closest checklist for the model-development parts. A reviewer will expect its items on data sources, outcome (label) definition, analytical methods including tuning and internal validation, performance measures with confidence intervals and subgroups, fairness, and open science. DECIDE-AI governs the next, live, physician-in-the-loop study. CONSORT-AI and SPIRIT-AI govern any later RCT. CLAIM is imaging-specific and does not apply. ## Path from offline evaluation to real-world evaluation for a clinician-supervised recommender, with examples

### Takeaway
The literature supports a staged path:
1. In silico fidelity (now).
2. Blinded expert rating or ranking of vignettes.
3. A silent or shadow-mode study measuring physician-model agreement (DECIDE-AI item 12).
4. A feasibility pilot with usability (SUS) and prespecified progression criteria.
5. A SPIRIT-AI/CONSORT-AI trial.

Physician edits are a valuable implicit label but a biased one (automation bias, trust bias). They should be collected as relative preferences, ideally with a blinded or randomised display.

### Cited Findings
**Staging frameworks**
- Park et al.'s phases run from Phase 0 discovery, through Phase 1 technical performance and safety (in silico optimisation, scenario-based usability, expert review) and Phase 2 efficacy and side effects (A/B or crossover, guideline-concordant decisions, trust and reliance), to Phase 3 RCT and Phase 4 monitoring. — [Park et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7660958/)
- Silent trial: clinicians are blinded to predictions. It exposed an AUC fall from 0.90 to 0.50 from data drift and informed the UI design before any trial. — [Kwong et al. 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36052317/)
- Feasibility versus pilot: "A feasibility study asks whether something can be done, should we proceed with it, and if so, how"; a pilot conducts a future study "or part of a future study ... on a smaller scale". — [Eldridge SM, Lancaster GA, Campbell MJ, et al. 2016, *PLoS One* 11(3):e0150205, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0150205](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26978655/)

**Expert or dietitian agreement**
- Blinded multi-profession rating of AI versus centre-made diet plans on five criteria, including hypothetical patients: no significant differences, and experts mostly could not identify the AI plan. — [Kim et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38577160/)
- A chronic-disease dietary recommender (DIETOS) was "validated for usability for both patients and specialists" and for "correctness of the profiling and recommendation" with 20 CKD patients and 20 age-matched controls, reporting high specificity and sensitivity. — [Agapito G, et al. 2018, *Comput Methods Programs Biomed* 153:93-104, doi:10.1016/j.cmpb.2017.10.014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29157465/)
- Reliability statistics: a guideline on selecting and reporting intraclass correlation coefficients (bibliographic record verified; content not read). — [Koo TK, Li MY 2016, *J Chiropr Med* 15(2):155-163, doi:10.1016/j.jcm.2016.02.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcm.2016.02.012)

**Physician edits, overrides and use as implicit labels**
- DECIDE-AI item 12 asks how often users agreed with the AI, "when and why they departed from it", and cases where users changed their decision. — [Vasey et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9116198/)
- Draft-utilisation rate as the primary outcome of a 5-week prospective pilot (162 clinicians): mean AI-draft use was 20%, with no time savings but lower task load and exhaustion scores. — [Garcia P, Ma SP, Shah S, et al. 2024, *JAMA Network Open* 7(3):e243201, doi:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2024.3201](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38506805/)
- Clinician responses as the evaluation in a 16-month prospective ML CDS deployment: staff rated every alert; 85% were valid, 80% useful, and 43% led to changed orders. — [Segal G, Segev A, Brom A, et al. 2019, *JAMIA* 26(12):1560-1565, doi:10.1093/jamia/ocz135](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31390471/)
- Edit-based evaluation of AI output: for automatic contours, standard geometric measures correlated poorly with clinicians' editing time, while "added path length" (the amount of editing) correlated best and was "the most representative measure for clinical usefulness". — [Vaassen F, et al. 2020, *Phys Imaging Radiat Oncol* 13:1-6, doi:10.1016/j.phro.2019.12.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.phro.2019.12.001)
- Learning recommendations from clinician behaviour carries a caveat: "ongoing evaluation is necessary to discern common behaviors from 'correct' ones". — [Chen JH et al. 2016, OrderRex](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocv091)
- Implicit feedback is biased (trust bias, quality bias); relative preferences are "reasonably accurate on average". — [Joachims et al. 2005](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/people/tj/publications/joachims_etal_05a.pdf)
- Automation bias in CDS, and design mitigators (advice position, confidence display, information versus recommendation). — [Goddard et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21685142/)

**Usability**
- The SUS benchmark of 68 holds for digital health apps (68.05 excluding physical-activity apps). — [Hyzy et al. 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35980732/)

**User studies and trials of diet recommenders**
- A meal recommender was evaluated in a field study of 227 users and a 60-person user study, which reported a 42.63% higher recommendation acceptance rate. — [Yang L, et al. 2017, Yum-me, *ACM TOIS* 36(1), doi:10.1145/3072614](https://doi.org/10.1145/3072614)
- An ML diet algorithm was tested in "a blinded randomized controlled dietary intervention", after development in an 800-person cohort (46,898 meals) and validation in an independent 100-person cohort. — [Zeevi D, et al. 2015, *Cell* 163(5):1079-1094, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2015.11.001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26590418/)

### Inferences
- The project's own description says physician approval is a workflow gate, not validation. The literature agrees: approval rates on a model-ranked list are inflated by automation and trust bias (Goddard; Joachims). Edit data are most informative when (a) the physician's independent choice is captured before the model's list is shown (silent-trial style), or (b) the display order is randomised, and (c) edits are converted to pairwise preferences ("dish A was moved above dish B").
- Agreement should be judged against human-human agreement. If two physicians or dietitians agree with each other at, say, RBO x, a model reaching x is at the human ceiling. This needs a multi-rater design and ICC or an equivalent statistic (Koo & Li).
- The first study where the three rankers (formula, label function g, trained model) can differ in a meaningful way is the expert study. In the synthetic table the model is near g by construction; against experts, g itself may be wrong. Reporting g's agreement with experts is the most informative single number the next paper can produce.

### Gaps
- I found no published study that used physician edits to diet plans as training labels. The closest analogues are clinician-response measures in CDS and AI drafting (Segal; Garcia), edit-effort measures in radiotherapy (Vaassen) and order recommenders (OrderRex).
- No verified source was found on sample-size planning for expert-agreement studies of ranked lists; this needs a statistics reference before the protocol is written.
- No Bangladesh-specific pilot of a physician-supervised diet recommender was searched (outside scope).

## Synthesis: how to describe the synthetic-label evaluation honestly, how to defend it, the strongest criticism, and a concrete next-evaluation plan

### Takeaway
Describe the model table as a simulation study of emulation fidelity: how well each learner recovers a known, guideline-derived suitability function on synthetic archetypes it did not see. Defend it with simulation-study, metamodel and weak-supervision precedents, the archetype-grouped split and the monotone-shape guarantees. Concede that it says nothing about clinical appropriateness. Then run a staged evaluation in which the label function itself is tested against Bangladeshi physicians and dietitians.

### Cited Findings
**Suggested honest wording, with its supporting sources** (wording is an inference; sources support each element). For example: "Because no preference or outcome data exist, we ran a simulation study (ADEMP). The data-generating mechanism was a full factorial of synthetic archetypes; the estimand was the ranking implied by a hand-written suitability function derived from cited guidelines. Models were trained on noisy labels and scored against the noise-free function on archetypes withheld from training and tuning. The results measure how faithfully each model reproduces that function. They do not show that the rankings are clinically appropriate or preferred."
- Simulation studies are justified where "truth" is known from the generating process, and must report ADEMP. — [Morris et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30652356/)
- This matches metamodel validation (design of experiments, fit, performance assessment, verification). — [Degeling et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32428428/)

**Citations that defend the method**
- Rule- and heuristic-generated labels are a recognised training paradigm in general and clinical ML. — [Ratner et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.14778/3157794.3157797); [Wang Y et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30616584/); [Fries et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33795682/); [von Rueden et al.](https://doi.org/10.1109/TKDE.2021.3079836)
- Synthetic user profiles with guideline-encoded objectives have been used to evaluate a nutrition recommender. — [Papastratis et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38918477/)
- Archetype-grouped splitting mirrors the use case of new patients. — [Saeb et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28327985/)
- Monotone constraints encode known directions, support trust and block artefacts like asthma-lowers-risk. — [Gupta et al. 2016](https://jmlr.org/papers/v17/15-243.html); [Wang & Gupta 2020](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v108/wang20e.html); [Caruana et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1145/2783258.2788613)
- Algorithm-only offline evaluation is common in HRS research (about half to two-thirds of studies), so the stage is normal if labelled as such. — [De Croon et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34185014/); [Sun et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36656630/)

**The strongest criticisms a reviewer would make, with sources**
- *Circularity*: the model is trained and tested against the same hand-written function, so a high Spearman is expected and uninformative about validity. Synthetic data used "as if it is real" do not generalise. Rule-generated patients lack real deviations in care. Offline rankings can contradict online ones. — [van Breugel et al. 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/van-breugel23a.html); [Chen J et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30871520/); [Rossetti et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1145/2959100.2959176)
- *Unfair baselines*: an untuned, necessarily unconstrained GradientBoostingRegressor (no `monotonic_cst` in scikit-learn) and a LinearRegression that can only force all coefficients positive. Tuned simple baselines often match complex models. — [scikit-learn GBR docs](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.ensemble.GradientBoostingRegressor.html); [scikit-learn LinearRegression docs](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.linear_model.LinearRegression.html); [Ferrari Dacrema et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.06902)
- *Optimistic validation*: possible tuning leakage, and interpolation-only held-out archetypes. — [Varma & Simon 2006](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-7-91); [Roberts et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1111/ecog.02881)

**Concrete next-evaluation plan grounded in the cited work**
1. **Tighten the offline study (now, no participants).**
   - Report in ADEMP form (Morris).
   - Add leave-one-finding-combination-out, leave-one-intake-group-out and leave-items-out splits (Roberts).
   - Confirm test archetypes were untouched by the randomized search, or use nested CV (Varma & Simon).
   - Add a tuned, unconstrained HistGradientBoostingRegressor and an equally tuned linear baseline (Dacrema; scikit-learn docs).
   - Add nDCG@10 and RBO with per-archetype distributions and CIs by subgroup (Järvelin & Kekäläinen; Webber; TRIPOD+AI 12d/23a).
   - Table each constrained feature with its sign and guideline source.
   - Release code and the archetype generator (TRIPOD+AI 18e/18f). — [Morris](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30652356/); [Roberts](https://doi.org/10.1111/ecog.02881); [Varma & Simon](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-7-91); [Dacrema](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.06902); [Webber](https://doi.org/10.1145/1852102.1852106); [Järvelin](https://doi.org/10.1145/582415.582418); [TRIPOD+AI](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11019967/)
2. **Expert agreement study (Park Phase 1, "expert review").**
   - Physicians and dietitians in Bangladesh rank or rate top-10 food, recipe and exercise lists for a stratified sample of archetype vignettes, blind to source.
   - Lists are drawn from the trained model, the label function g, the formula and a dietitian-made list (Kim et al. design).
   - Measure expert-expert agreement (ICC; Koo & Li) and each source's agreement with the experts (RBO and nDCG against an expert consensus ranking).
   - Collect free-text reasons for disagreement, which are the main input for revising g. — [Kim et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38577160/); [Park et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7660958/); [Koo & Li 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcm.2016.02.012)
3. **Silent or shadow-mode study in the physician workflow.**
   - For real visits, physicians first draft their own selection without seeing the model's ranking, then see it (or see a randomised order).
   - Log agreement, edits and reasons, reported under DECIDE-AI items 10-14.
   - Convert edits into pairwise preferences (Joachims) and treat raw approval rates as biased (Goddard).
   - Precedents for clinician-response outcomes: Segal (valid, useful, changed orders); Garcia (utilisation rate as primary outcome); Vaassen (amount of editing tracks usefulness). These preference pairs are the first real labels for retraining. — [Kwong et al. 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36052317/); [DECIDE-AI](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9116198/); [Joachims et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1145/1076034.1076063); [Goddard et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21685142/); [Segal et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31390471/); [Garcia et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38506805/); [Vaassen et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.phro.2019.12.001)
4. **Feasibility pilot with patients.**
   - Prespecified progression criteria (recruitment, retention, daily-task adherence, physician time per approval, safety events) per the pilot/feasibility framework and the CONSORT pilot extension.
   - SUS for patients and physicians against the benchmark of 68 (Hyzy).
   - User-experience constructs (Knijnenburg; Pu).
   - Describe the intervention per CONSORT-EHEALTH/mERA subitems. — [Eldridge et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26978655/); [CONSORT pilot extension](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27777223/); [Hyzy et al. 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35980732/); [Knijnenburg et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11257-011-9118-4); [CONSORT-EHEALTH](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22209829/)
5. **Controlled evaluation (Park Phase 2-3).**
   - Crossover or randomised comparison of physician-plus-recommender against physician-alone plans, registered with a SPIRIT-AI protocol and reported with CONSORT-AI.
   - A blinded randomised dietary intervention design (Zeevi) is the template for outcome measures. — [Park et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7660958/); [SPIRIT-AI](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32908284/); [CONSORT-AI](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32908283/); [Zeevi et al. 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26590418/)

### Inferences
- The single most important reframing: the object that needs validating next is the label function g, not the model. The model's fidelity to g is already near ceiling (0.998/0.984). Every later study should report g, the formula and the model side by side against human judgement.
- The paper should answer "why ML if g exists?" explicitly. The defensible answer is that the trained ranker is the component built to absorb real physician-review labels (step 3) while keeping monotone guarantees. If that is not the plan, a reviewer may reasonably ask the authors to ship g.
- Concede up front that 168 archetypes is a small number of groups. Per-archetype CIs and worst-case archetypes will matter more to reviewers than the mean.
- Keep outcome words out of the text, consistent with the project's own writing rules. "Recovers the labelling function", "agreement with physicians" and "usability" are the defensible terms at each stage.

### Gaps
- No verified source gives sample sizes for multi-rater ranking-agreement studies or for silent trials of recommenders; a statistician or a methods reference is needed before the protocol.
- Whether the shipped model or g produces the live rankings, and whether the reported held-out archetypes were excluded from tuning, could not be confirmed from the brief; the paper must state both.
